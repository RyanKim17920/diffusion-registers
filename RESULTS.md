# Do loss-free register tokens help a masked diffusion language model?

Short answer, on synthetic Sudoku: **no**, under either of two register designs,
across 40 training runs. The more interesting finding is *why* — and that one of
the two designs produces registers the model cannot function without, while still
solving no more puzzles than a model that never had any.

All runs share architecture (6 layers, d=256, 8 heads, ~4.78M params), data,
optimizer and schedule. Only `K` and the seed vary. Runs sharing a seed see
byte-identical data order and identical masks/block plans, because those RNG
streams are seeded independently of `K` — so a per-seed paired comparison
cancels the run-to-run variation that otherwise dominates.

## Data

1,000,000 train / 20,000 val / 20,000 test unique-solution puzzles, clue counts
uniform over [24,45], generated by randomized-backtracking fill plus
uniqueness-verified hole digging. No solution grid appears in more than one
split, so the holdout tests generalisation rather than puzzle memorisation.
Verified by `src/verify_sudoku.py`, whose solution counter is written from
scratch and shares no code with the generator: all checks pass, including a
300-puzzle uniqueness spot-check.

## Experiment 1 — stateless registers

Registers re-read from the learned embedding table on **every forward pass**, so
they never carry anything between denoising steps. Decoding is free-order: 81
steps, each revealing the single highest-confidence masked cell anywhere.
100k steps, 3 seeds per arm.

| K | val CE | test exact | test exact (≤27 clues) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | .0178 ± .0020 | .9715 ± .0046 | .8766 ± .0148 |
| 1 | .0197 ± .0009 | .9700 ± .0057 | .8713 ± .0164 |
| 4 | .0186 ± .0021 | .9719 ± .0023 | .8770 ± .0102 |
| 16 | .0210 ± .0015 | .9719 ± .0016 | .8746 ± .0035 |
| 64 | .0228 ± .0025 | .9680 ± .0012 | .8640 ± .0036 |

No arm beats the baseline. Validation CE rises nearly monotonically with K
(.0178 → .0228 end to end, ~2σ); each individual delta sits inside the seed
noise, so no single one is significant, but the ordering across five independent
K values points one way, and K=64 also loses on the hard bucket.

### Why: the pathology registers are meant to fix is absent here

ViT registers are motivated by high-norm artifact tokens — ordinary tokens the
model repurposes as global scratch. If that is the mechanism, adding registers
should *drain* high-norm outliers out of the text tokens. Measuring the K=0
token-norm profile is what tests this, and it is the comparison that matters:

| K | mean tok norm (L5) | max tok norm (L5) | outlier frac (>3× median), L4 / L5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 102.3 | 210.2 | .0008 / .0044 |
| 1 | 90.1 | 191.7 | .0116 / .0009 |
| 4 | 108.6 | 218.5 | .0010 / .0024 |
| 16 | 95.1 | 217.9 | .0148 / .0020 |
| 64 | 95.7 | 208.7 | .0020 / .0020 |

**The baseline has essentially no high-norm outlier tokens** (under 0.5% above
3× the median at any layer; max/mean only ~1.8×), and registers drain nothing —
K>0 token norms are indistinguishable from K=0, and K=16 has *more* L4 outliers
than K=0. There is no artifact for a register to take over, which explains the
null far better than "registers don't work".

Consistent with that, the text stream ignores them: text→register attention sits
at or below the uniform baseline `K/(163+K)` at every layer past L0 (e.g. at
K=16, .0083–.0905 against a .0894 baseline), while register norms still grow to
~2.6× the token norm — high-norm, low-bandwidth sinks.

## Experiment 2 — registers carried across a generation block

Registers persist across the denoising steps **within** a generation block and
reset at each block boundary, so their lifetime is one block. Decoding becomes
semi-autoregressive (9 blocks of 9 cells; the highest-confidence masked cell *of
the current block* is revealed each step). Training unrolls a whole block with
gradient through the carry, so a step is 9 forward passes. Registers still have
no prediction target and no direct loss — the carry gradient from later steps in
the same block is the only path to them. 20k steps, 3 seeds (8 for K=0 and K=64).

Block-constrained decoding is far harder than free order — exact-solve drops from
0.97 to ~0.15 — which removes the ceiling and gives ~90 points of dynamic range.

| K | seeds | val CE | test exact | paired Δ vs K=0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 8 | .0679 ± .0031 | .1440 ± .0167 | — |
| 1 | 3 | .0664 ± .0032 | .1556 ± .0165 | +.0060 ± .0112 |
| 4 | 3 | .0670 ± .0027 | .1499 ± .0152 | +.0003 ± .0250 |
| 16 | 3 | .0681 ± .0026 | .1436 ± .0195 | −.0060 ± .0299 |
| 64 | 8 | .0668 ± .0028 | .1551 ± .0204 | +.0111 ± .0240 |

K=64 paired over 8 seeds: **t=1.31, df=7, p≈0.23**, 7/8 seeds positive (sign
test p≈0.07). Not established. At n=3 this same arm looked like +.0123 ± .0067
(t=3.16, p≈0.087); adding five seeds more than tripled the paired sd (.0067 →
.0240) and the effect fell apart. A small-n variance underestimate, caught only
by running more seeds.

### The carry is load-bearing — and that is not the same as useful

| K | normal | no_carry | zero | shuffle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | .1432 ± .0212 | **.0000 ± .0000** | .0072 ± .0116 | .1432 ± .0212 |
| 4 | .1478 ± .0121 | **.0000 ± .0000** | .0008 ± .0010 | .1493 ± .0123 |
| 16 | .1350 ± .0160 | **.0000 ± .0000** | .0015 ± .0010 | .1347 ± .0168 |
| 64 | .1540 ± .0101 | **.0017 ± .0020** | .0207 ± .0303 | .1563 ± .0106 |

`no_carry` re-initialises the registers every step — severing the scratchpad
channel while leaving their content and the model untouched. It takes every
register model to **zero solved puzzles**, in every seed. The carried state is a
genuine running state: it evolves smoothly (‖Δ‖ 9.3 → 3.6 across a block, cos to
previous ≈ .97) and drifts off its initialisation (cos to init .39 → ~0).

And yet none of these models beats K=0. **The trained model co-adapts to the
carry until it cannot function without it, without solving more puzzles than a
model that never had one.** Necessity and usefulness come apart cleanly here: an
ablation-only study would report "registers are critical" and be badly wrong. The
K=0 arm is the entire difference between those two conclusions.

`shuffle` is a no-op at every K, including K=16 and K=64 — permuting which
register occupies which slot costs nothing. The registers hold a
permutation-invariant aggregate, a bag of state with no division of labour, which
is also why adding more of them does not help.

## Experiment 3 — text (wikitext-103), stateless registers

51.5M params, 8 layers, d=512, seq 1024, 119.7M GPT-2 BPE train tokens, 50k
steps, K in {0,16}. Metric is validation denoising CE (no ceiling). Seed noise
here is far tighter than Sudoku: K=0 sd 0.0055 nats over 8 seeds.

**Text has the pathology Sudoku lacked.** This is what makes it a real test:

| | Sudoku K=0 | Text K=0 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| outlier fraction (>3x median) | .0008 – .0044 | **.026 – .038** |
| max / mean token norm | ~1.8x | **~5.2x** |

The mean argmax position is ~500 of 1024 (uniform), so these are
content-dependent artifact tokens, not a fixed positional sink.

**Registers absorb them.** Outlier fraction roughly halves at every layer
(L4: .0376 -> .0167) and max token norm drops ~40% (L6: 1914 -> 1138). Unlike
Sudoku, the text stream reads them: text->register attention is .11–.42 against
a .0154 uniform baseline (7–27x above chance), and the registers are not
themselves high-norm (~1.1x token norm). `shuffle` is still an exact no-op, so
it remains a permutation-invariant bag of state.

**The perplexity win did NOT survive more seeds.** At 3 seeds it was -0.0086
nats (3/3 seeds, t=-4.76, df=2). At 8 seeds it is **-0.0049 nats, t=-1.24,
df=7, p~0.22** — not significant. The paired sd tripled (0.0031 -> 0.0112) and
seed 7 flipped sign (+0.0204); 7/8 still favour registers (sign test p~0.07),
which is suggestive and nothing more.

This is the second time the same failure mode appeared: the Sudoku K=64 arm
also looked significant at n=3 (t=3.16) and dissolved at n=8. Treat no n=3
result in this project as real.

The perplexity claim is therefore withdrawn. It had already been demoted on
cost grounds — +6% wall clock spent on extra training steps buys ~0.011-0.019
nats, more than registers ever delivered — so the thesis does not rest on it.
What the thesis rests on is outlier reduction, which is a ~30x larger effect,
and which must now face the same n=8 test before it is claimed.

Cost: wall clock +6.0%, analytic FLOPs +2.0% (linear +1.56%, attention +3.15%);
the gap is implementation overhead, since 1024+16=1040 breaks tensor-core
tiling. Spending +6% on more steps instead is worth ~0.011–0.019 nats at
loss-compute exponents 0.04–0.07 — more than the registers buy. So
wall-clock-matched they plausibly lose and FLOP-matched they win narrowly.
A direct compute-matched control (K=0 at 53k steps vs K=16 at 50k) is running.

**No instability to prevent.** Zero grad-norm spikes above 5x median in any of
the 16 runs; K=16 is marginally smoother (mean grad norm 0.821 vs 0.885) but
neither arm is near unstable. The likelier practical payoff is the 40% drop in
max activation norm — activation outliers are the main obstacle to INT8/FP8
quantization, and that effect dwarfs the perplexity change.

## Limitations

- **Scale.** The text result is a single model size (51.5M). A d=256 / d=768
  ladder at fixed data is running to show whether the gap grows or shrinks; the
  headline 0.0086 nats is too small to extrapolate from one point.
- **The text CE win is provisional** at n=3 paired seeds, for exactly the reason
  the Sudoku K=64 arm was: n=3 underestimates the paired sd.
- **The hard-Sudoku control is weak.** Targeting clue counts [20,24] produced a
  set piled at the top of the range (24: 206831, 23: 78292, 22: 13956, 21: 906,
  20: 15 of 300k), because digs targeting 20–22 almost always fail the uniqueness
  check. Random digging cannot reach low clue counts at all (0 of 80 attempts at
  [18,21]). It was not used for any conclusion; block decoding supplied the
  difficulty instead.
- **Block size fixed at 9.** Register lifetime equals block length, so block size
  and the amount a register could usefully carry are confounded.
- **No_carry is out of distribution.** It proves the trained model depends on the
  carry, not that the carry computes anything useful — which is precisely why the
  K=0 comparison carries the weight.

## Reproducing

```bash
scripts/launch.sh --ks "0 1 4 16 64" --seeds "0 1 2" --tag phase1          # stateless
scripts/launch.sh --ks "0 1 4 16 64" --seeds "0 1 2" --tag blk \
    --script train_block.py --block_size 9 --steps 20000                   # carried
.venv/bin/python src/compare.py --glob 'blk_*'
.venv/bin/python src/summarize_block_analysis.py
```
